# Comments on the draft National Payment System Bill, 2026

Draft for the owner (FS-376, FS-369). The submission goes on CBK's own
template (five columns, below) to **paymentslawreview@centralbank.go.ke**,
or by hand to the Office of the Governor, **on or before Friday 9 October
2026**. The template is filled from this file; this file is the one to
edit.

Every "Provision" quotes the Bill as published on the CBK website in
September 2026. Every "Proposed revision" is the exact wording we ask for,
as the template requires.

**Our interest, stated plainly.** Dibon builds payment, reconciliation and
core-banking software for banks, Saccos, MFIs and businesses. It never
holds customer funds. As drafted, the Bill could require it to hold a
payment system operator licence. The comments say so, and argue for
proportion rather than exemption.

## The comments

### 1 · Clause 2, "payment gateway"

**Provision.** "payment gateway" means a technology infrastructure to
route and facilitate processing of a payment transaction without any
involvement in handling of funds;

**Proposed revision.** "payment gateway" means a technology infrastructure
that routes payment instructions between two or more payment service
providers or payment systems on behalf of merchants, without any
involvement in handling of funds, but does not include a technology
service provider;

and insert: "technology service provider" means a person that provides
software, hosting or related technical services to a payment service
provider, a payment system operator, a bank or a microfinance bank, or to
a payer or payee for use with that person's own accounts, payment
channels or payment instruments, and that does not hold, receive, transfer
or settle funds;

**Rationale.** As drafted, any software that sends a payment request or
receives a payment confirmation on behalf of a business falls within
"payment gateway", including invoicing, accounting, ERP and reconciliation
software. Each would need a payment system operator licence with KSh 10
million in core capital, although none holds funds and each acts through
channels that are already licensed: the bank's or the business's own
paybill and accounts. This raises the cost of local software, favours
large incumbents over Kenyan developers, and duplicates oversight that
already applies through the licensed institutions. Separating gateways
that route between payment service providers from technology providers
keeps licensing where the risk is, and supports the Bill's objects of
innovation and competition.

### 2 · Third Schedule, minimum capital for a Payment Gateway

**Provision.** Payment System Operator, Payment Gateway: Ksh. 10 million.

**Proposed revision.** Payment System Operator, Payment Gateway: Ksh. 10
million; or Ksh. 2 million where the gateway routes payment instructions
for fewer than such number of merchants, or below such transaction value,
as the Central Bank may prescribe, together with professional indemnity
insurance as the Central Bank may prescribe.

**Rationale.** Minimum capital protects customers whose funds a provider
holds. A gateway that, by definition, has no involvement in handling funds
carries operational risk, not risk to customer funds. That risk is better
met by proportionate requirements: insurance, cybersecurity standards,
business continuity and the Central Bank's inspection powers. A threshold
for smaller gateways lets Kenyan start-ups enter the market and grow into
the full requirement.

### 3 · Clause 4(1)(k) and (o), who the Act applies to

**Provision.** (k) processes, stores or shares data on behalf of a payment
service provider or a user of payment services; … (o) engages in ancillary
services that support the provision of a payment system or payment
service.

**Proposed revision.** Insert a new subclause (3): "A technology service
provider to which this Act applies by reason only of paragraph (k) or (o)
of subsection (1) shall not be required to obtain a licence under Part II,
and shall comply with such requirements on cybersecurity, data protection,
record keeping and access by the Central Bank as the Central Bank may
prescribe in regulations."

**Rationale.** Paragraphs (k) and (o) bring into the Act every business
whose software handles payment data for a customer, including accounting,
payroll, ERP and reconciliation software used by ordinary businesses. The
Bill does not say which obligations then apply to them. Stating that such
providers are not licensed, and naming the requirements that do apply,
gives certainty to the providers and to the institutions that contract
them, without weakening the Central Bank's oversight.

### 4 · Clause 30(1), outsourcing

**Provision.** (1) Where a payment service provider or payment system
operator intends to outsource any of its operation functions to a third
party, it shall seek the prior written approval of the Central Bank.

**Proposed revision.** (1) Where a payment service provider or payment
system operator intends to outsource a material operational function to a
third party, it shall seek the prior written approval of the Central Bank,
and it shall notify the Central Bank at least fourteen days before
outsourcing any other operational function. (1A) The Central Bank shall
determine an application under subsection (1) within sixty days of
receiving a complete application.

**Rationale.** "Any of its operation functions" covers routine services
such as SMS delivery, software support and hosting. Prior approval for
every arrangement would slow the adoption of secure cloud services and
local software, and add to the Central Bank's workload without reducing
risk. A material/non-material distinction, with approval for the first and
notice for the second, matches the approach in the Central Bank's draft
Prudential Guideline on Third Party Service Providers published in
September 2026, so banks and payment service providers would work under
one regime. A decision period gives institutions certainty when planning
projects.

### 5 · Clause 7(5), time to decide an application

**Provision.** (5) The Central Bank shall process the application under
sections 5(1) and 6(1) within the prescribed period after the submission
of a complete application.

**Proposed revision.** (5) The Central Bank shall determine an application
under sections 5(1) and 6(1) within ninety days after the submission of a
complete application.

**Rationale.** A fixed period in the Act gives applicants, investors and
their customers certainty, and is consistent with the fourteen-day period
the Bill already sets for giving reasons for a refusal (clause 7(6)(b)).

### 6 · Clause 79(1), transition

**Provision.** (1) Upon the commencement of this Act, any person providing
payment services shall, within one year of the commencement, comply with
the provisions of this Act.

**Proposed revision.** (1) Upon the commencement of this Act, any person
providing payment services shall, within one year of the commencement,
comply with the provisions of this Act, save that a person carrying on a
business that did not require a licence before the commencement of this
Act shall apply for a licence within eighteen months of the commencement,
and may continue that business until its application is determined.

**Rationale.** The Bill creates licence categories, such as payment
gateways, that did not exist before. Businesses in those categories serve
Saccos, microfinance institutions and merchants today. Allowing them to
continue while their applications are decided protects those customers
from interruption, and gives the Central Bank time to process a new class
of applications.

### 7 · Clause 74, regulatory sandbox

**Provision.** (1) The Central Bank may, by regulations, establish a
regulatory sandbox framework for purposes of governing the manner in which
a person may obtain limited access to the national payment system to test
innovative payment system and settlement services without necessarily
obtaining a license under this Act.

**Proposed revision.** (1) The Central Bank shall, within six months of
the commencement of this Act, by regulations establish a regulatory
sandbox framework for purposes of governing the manner in which a person,
including a technology service provider, may obtain limited access to the
national payment system to test innovative payment system and settlement
services without necessarily obtaining a license under this Act.

**Rationale.** We welcome the sandbox. Making it mandatory, with a
timeline, and open to technology providers lets new products be tested
with the Central Bank before full licensing, which matters most in the
new categories the Bill creates.

### 8 · Clause 29, open finance (support)

**Provision.** (1) Each payment service provider or payment system
operator shall use systems that are capable of securely sharing customer
data with third parties for open finance purposes.

**Proposed revision.** Retain subclauses (1) to (3), and add: "(4) The
Central Bank shall publish common technical and security standards for
the interfaces used to share customer data under this section."

**Rationale.** We support open finance. Common published standards let
every institution and provider build once, securely, instead of to a
different interface at each institution, which lowers cost and widens
access.
