# Decisions, 1100 to 1199

19 decisions, in id order. How an entry is written, and why, is
[the index](../decisions.md).

## FS-1109 · Repayments and disbursements through every rail

*Entry written 2026-11-05 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** every payment rail carries money both ways: a borrower may
  repay through any live rail, mobile money or bank, and be disbursed to
  through any live rail, to the wallet or account they nominated and the
  institution approved.
- **Requires:** docs/templates/delivery-channels.md "Every rail carries money both ways"; docs/templates/ict-policy.md "customers keep repaying by mobile money or bank transfer".
- **Argued (owner):** "we should accept repayments and disbursements via all channels, not that mpesa is restricted to repayments".
- **Argued against (inferred):** one direction per rail, repayments by
  mobile money and disbursements by bank, which sends a borrower without
  a bank account to open one before they can be paid, and leaves a
  business borrower paying by transfer outside the platform.
- **Costs (inferred):** each rail's outgoing side set up and funded for
  each institution (an M-Pesa B2C shortcode with its float, each bank's
  payment authority), and each direction reconciled daily.

*Entry written 2026-11-05 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** R1 reaches borrowers by SMS, email and the customer pages;
  WhatsApp is listed among the channels not offered at launch, and comes
  later as a new channel, with thirty days' notice to CBK.
- **Requires:** docs/templates/delivery-channels.md "An institution that uses WhatsApp adds its row"; docs/deployments/roan-capital-production.md "Decided: not in R1".
- **Argued (owner):** "let's leave whatsapp out of r1".
- **Argued against (inferred):** WhatsApp at launch for remote loan
  processing, as Roan Capital had agreed (FS-737), before Meta's policy
  on lenders' messages is verified (FS-165) and its route chosen (FS-161).
- **Costs (inferred):** borrowers who prefer WhatsApp get SMS and email
  at first; adding it later needs CBK's notice, Meta-approved templates
  and a review of the data leaving Kenya.

## FS-1131 · One transaction model for every component

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** one model of a money movement, every field defined once in
  a dictionary and mapped by each component: a header fixed at creation,
  a body fixed at approval under its hash, a footer only appended to.
  Money as integer minor units and an ISO 4217 code; the amount as
  instructed or received, with an amount type (send or receive) and the
  charges and settled amount apart; identifiers as a scheme and a value,
  international first; identifier, address and alias kept apart, an
  alias resolved before approval and again before sending, Mojaloop's
  party types as the alias types; links pointing back from a closed
  list, each leaving its forward fact on the target's footer; the model
  versioned MAJOR.MINOR, transactions never migrated; the txid a UUIDv7
  minted once by the first of our components to know of the movement,
  every other party's id kept by issuer and scheme; ISO 20022 codes,
  three dates, tax as its own lines, rounding only by product term.
  Decisions 1 to 4 agreed under FS-1123, 5 to 10 here.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §3 "Links point back; the target's footer records them"; docs/transaction-fields.md §4 "OriginatorConversationID"; docs/ids.md "Transaction fields".
- **Argued (owner):** "go on 5 to 10, with the amount type".
- **Argued against (inferred):** each component keeping its own fields
  and names (Daraja's, Fineract's, Odoo's), reconciled by mapping each
  pair, which leaves amount, fee and reference meaning different things
  in different places; or our own codes for statuses and reasons in
  place of ISO 20022's.
- **Costs (inferred):** every money service writes and validates the
  shape, keeps its component map current, and adopts the spec when next
  revised; old versions read through converters kept as long as the
  records.

## FS-1137 · A transaction's position, computed; the link rules run each night

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** a reversal or refund never changes the original's status,
  which stays the rail's fact; what they leave of it is its position
  (settled, partly refunded, refunded, partly reversed, reversed),
  computed from its footer's forward facts and shown beside the status,
  never stored. Reconciliation runs the link rules over each day's
  transactions every night, and a failure is a break.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §5 "The position"; docs/specs/recon.md "The transaction model's night checks"; docs/transaction-fields.md "Computed, never stored".
- **Argued (owner):** "go with the position and night check".
- **Argued against (inferred):** a reversed status appended to the
  original, which cannot say how much of it was reversed, breaks the
  match against the rail's statement, and has two services writing one
  record's status; or the link rules checked only in the docs and in
  each service's tests.
- **Costs (inferred):** each screen and report computes the position;
  recon carries a further night check and its tickets.

## FS-1142 · The transaction travels in the events we already have

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** each existing money event in the catalogue carries, as its
  data, the transaction or the footer entries it appends, validated
  against the schema at the version its `dataschema` names; no family of
  transaction events beside them.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §6 "no family of transaction events beside them".
- **Argued (owner):** "Which events carry the transaction: agreed".
- **Argued against (inferred):** a new family of transaction events
  beside the domain events, which doubles every money event and leaves
  consumers to join the two.
- **Costs (inferred):** each money event's schema is written on the
  transaction model, and changes with it.

## FS-1143 · The reference's check digits: ISO 7064 MOD 97-10

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** a transaction's short reference is nine of Crockford's
  base-32 symbols and two check digits by ISO 7064 MOD 97-10, the method
  IBANs (ISO 13616) and LEIs (ISO 17442) use: eleven characters.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §3 "two check digits by ISO 7064 MOD 97-10"; docs/transaction-fields.md "ISO 7064 MOD 97-10".
- **Argued (owner):** "go with mod 97-10".
- **Argued against (inferred):** ISO 7064's hybrid method stretched to
  Crockford's 32 symbols, which none of the standard's named systems is;
  or its MOD 37,36, whose single check character can be a letter
  Crockford's alphabet leaves out.
- **Costs (inferred):** one character of the reference given to a second
  check digit, leaving nine for the reference itself, unique within an
  institution and checked when minted.

## FS-1144 · A number's operator: assumed from its code, confirmed with the operator

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** the operator behind a mobile number is taken from its
  national destination code, as the Communications Authority assigns
  them, and confirmed with that operator over our own connections to it
  before money goes to the number; a number the operator does not
  confirm holds the payout.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §3 "over our own connections to it"; docs/transaction-fields.md "Kenya's mobile numbers".
- **Argued (owner):** "we'll assume based on ndc, but run a verification check with the telcos, as we're supposed to be connected to them anyway".
- **Argued against (inferred):** the code alone, wrong for every ported
  number; or a lookup alone, with nothing to fall back on when it is
  unavailable.
- **Costs (inferred):** a query to an operator before each payout to a
  wallet, and each operator's query agreed and run; the authority's plan
  read again as blocks are assigned.

## FS-1149 · A transaction's fields in groups

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** each of the header, body and footer holds its fields in
  groups one level deep, each answering one question: the header's id,
  what and origin; the body's money, parties and terms; the footer's
  progress, settlement and books; with the model version, the links, the
  approval, the ids, the forward facts and the changes standing beside
  them. Kept at model 1.0, as nothing is built or published under it.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §4 "Its groups, and what they hold"; docs/transaction-fields.md "The groups".
- **Argued (owner):** "i think the header and footer would benefit from grouping", then "all three".
- **Argued against (inferred):** every part's fields side by side, thirteen
  in the header and twelve in the footer, which reads as a list rather
  than as answers; or groups nested deeper, which make every path longer
  for little more order.
- **Costs (inferred):** longer paths everywhere (`footer.settlement.settled`),
  the dictionary renumbered once before release, and each component map
  written against the grouped paths.

## FS-1150 · A source on every transaction; more than one account as a parent and its parts

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** every transaction names the account its money leaves from
  (`body.parties.source`) beside the one it lands in, so each moves money
  from one account to one account, as a rail does; money out names its
  source before approval. Money paid from, or to, more than one account
  is one parent, which posts the whole and moves no money itself, and a
  part per account, each its own instruction; one approval covers the
  plan, the parent settles only when every part has, and a failed part
  is replaced, never edited.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §3 "Money out names its source"; docs/transaction-fields.md "parties.source".
- **Argued (owner):** "how do we support multiple payer accounts?", then "go ahead".
- **Argued against (inferred):** one transaction with several sources,
  which no rail can carry as one instruction and recon cannot match to
  one statement line; or the paying account left out, so a payout cannot
  be matched to the institution's own statement.
- **Costs (inferred):** a source chosen for every payout before approval;
  the approval engine approving a plan as one request; a parent whose
  status waits on its parts.

## FS-1152 · Payments and transfers in the transaction model

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** one new kind, payment: money out of a customer's account to
  someone else. A withdrawal goes to the account holder; a transfer stays
  within the institution's books, between any two of its accounts; a
  deposit comes in from anyone. A customer's own instruction is approved
  by the customer, stepped up, and recorded by the approval engine; a
  payment is screened for sanctions and limits before it goes. Merchant
  collections and escrow are profiles of the same kinds when their
  phases come; the institution's own operating payments stay the back
  office's.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §2 "Deposit, withdrawal, payment, transfer"; docs/transaction-fields.md "EDUC".
- **Argued (owner):** "how do we include payments/transfers in the model?", then "go ahead".
- **Argued against (inferred):** payments folded into withdrawals, which
  loses who received the money and the screening a third party needs; or
  a kind per product (a bill payment, a merchant payment, an escrow
  release), which multiplies the kinds without changing the shape.
- **Costs (inferred):** sanctions and limits checked before every
  payment, and a purpose code chosen for it.

## FS-1156 · The rails' security gaps closed

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** the rails reach out only through one egress gateway, to each
  rail's own hosts; each callback endpoint has a secret path, a rate
  limit and refuses a replay; every rail credential has a rotation
  schedule, and one that can pay anyone is released only to the rails'
  identity and usable only from registered addresses; every rail setting
  that could redirect or hide money changes only through the approval
  engine, two people at least; each paying account has float and
  velocity limits.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/rails.md §3 "The rails reach out only to the rails"; docs/specs/rails.md §3 "A rail setting changes only through the approval engine"; docs/specs/approval-engine.md "rail.setting".
- **Argued (owner):** "close the gaps".
- **Argued against (inferred):** trusting the default-deny network and
  OpenBao alone, which leaves a compromised service free to call out, a
  changed callback address unnoticed, and a float drained by payouts
  each valid on its own.
- **Costs (inferred):** an egress gateway to run; each rail's hosts and
  credentials kept current; a second person for every rail setting.

## FS-1158 · Float limits at several thresholds, each with its alerts

*Entry written 2026-11-09 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** every account the institution pays from has levels (top up,
  low, critical, floor) and velocity limits by the hour and the day, each
  with what happens and who is told: payouts go on, then wait for a
  person above a set size, then wait at the floor, never rejected; a
  drain past a velocity limit waits for a person and calls on-call. The
  levels are the institution's, set as rail settings; the defaults are in
  hours of its usual payouts.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/rails.md §6 "Float thresholds".
- **Argued (owner):** "i like float limits extend it for multiple thresholds and alerts".
- **Argued against (inferred):** one low-balance alert, which says the
  float is low only once it is too late, and says nothing of a drain.
- **Costs (inferred):** balances read after every payout and on a
  schedule; each institution's finance role named for each level.

## FS-1159 · A callback address's token, a filter and not a proof

*Entry written 2026-11-10 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** each callback address holds an unguessable token of at least
  128 random bits, given to the rail only when the address is
  registered; masked in every log, rotated as each rail allows, replaced
  by a rail's signature or mutual TLS wherever it offers one. A request
  without it is refused at the gateway; whether a callback counts is
  still decided by its proof.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/rails.md §3 "unguessable token of at least 128 random bits".
- **Argued (owner):** "FS-1159: agreed".
- **Argued against (inferred):** the token taken as proof, when an
  address can reach a log; or no token, leaving the endpoint open to
  anyone who finds it.
- **Costs (inferred):** each token kept and rotated like a credential;
  Safaricom asked when a C2B address must change.

## FS-1160 · One shape for every limit

*Entry written 2026-11-10 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** every transaction rule and velocity limit has one shape,
  financial crime's (FC-16): a measure, over a scope, in a window,
  against a threshold, an amount or a multiple of the usual for that type
  of day; the rails' velocity limits on a paying account are rules of it,
  not a mechanism of their own.
- **Requires:** docs/modules/financial-crime.md "Every rule has one shape"; docs/specs/rails.md §3 "financial crime's transaction rules (FC-16) scoped to the paying".
- **Argued (owner):** "Velocity: amount, count, type per cycle. how else can we generalize?", then "go ahead with all three".
- **Argued against (inferred):** velocity limits in the rails beside
  financial crime's rules, two engines saying different things about one
  payment.
- **Costs (inferred):** the rule engine counts by more scopes and windows,
  and keeps a usual for every type of day.

## FS-1161 · Preparing for peak days

*Entry written 2026-11-10 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** peak days are a type of day on each institution's calendar,
  each with its expected multiple from history; before each, a load test
  above it, capacity raised on a schedule, each rail asked to raise its
  rate, the float funded, raised limits that revert by themselves, and no
  change; each rail paced to its agreed rate, the rest queued in order;
  a review after, setting the next multiple.
- **Requires:** docs/runbooks/peak-and-slow-days.md; docs/specs/rails.md §3 "Each rail is paced to what it takes"; docs/modules/core.md "Peak days and slow days".
- **Argued (owner):** "how do we prepare for a spike like black friday?", then "go ahead with all three".
- **Argued against (inferred):** capacity that only reacts, and limits
  that alert on every month-end.
- **Costs (inferred):** a load test before each peak; each rail's rate
  negotiated; a review after each.

## FS-1162 · Slow days as a signal

*Entry written 2026-11-10 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** every rule may look below the usual as well as above it;
  collections, channel requests and payouts far below the usual for the
  hour and type of day alert, and repayments below what the schedules
  expect are told to the institution's credit lead as an early warning;
  the expected slow days are on the calendar so they do not alert, and
  are preferred for changes.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/rails.md §10 "Collections far below the usual"; docs/runbooks/peak-and-slow-days.md "A slow reading".
- **Argued (owner):** "also include slow days as below normal traffic is also an interesting data point", then "go ahead with all three".
- **Argued against (inferred):** alerts only on too much, which leaves a
  silent failure, a rail's callbacks lost, to be found at the night's
  reconciliation.
- **Costs (inferred):** a usual kept for every hour and type of day; a
  credit lead named to receive the early warning.

## FS-1163 · The transaction model stretched to every product

*Entry written 2026-11-10 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** the seven gaps found against the product landscape closed
  without changing the model's shape: kinds dividend and waiver, a link
  recovers and a party kind group; the mandate a pull runs under and the
  day asked for; holds as a companion record; two currencies as a parent
  with its exchange and a leg in each; cards authorised then captured,
  and chargebacks; investments by units and price, with subscription,
  redemption and distribution; records observed in books not the
  institution's with us. Designed now, built in each product's phase.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §2 "Hold"; docs/transaction-fields.md §6 "Holds"; docs/transaction-fields.md "exchange".
- **Argued (owner):** "implement all 7 gaps".
- **Argued against (inferred):** leaving each product to extend the model
  when it is built, which is how the components' own names drifted apart
  before the model.
- **Costs (inferred):** a larger model to keep, and its dictionary
  renumbered once more before release.

## FS-1164 · Treasury, a function of its own

*Entry written 2026-11-10 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** treasury, where the institution's own money sits and how
  many hours of float it has, is a function of its own (RO-09): it owns
  each paying account's float levels and top-ups, which the rails
  enforce; its movements between the institution's own accounts are a
  kind of their own, treasury, always approved, never a customer's money.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/transactions.md §2 "Treasury"; docs/specs/rails.md §3 "The levels and the top-ups are treasury's".
- **Argued (owner):** "i like the include and separation of treasury as it's a major function in fis".
- **Argued against (inferred):** float as a detail of the rails, and its
  top-ups as transfers like a customer's.
- **Costs (inferred):** a treasurer's role named at each institution, and
  a top-up approved like any movement out.

## FS-1167 · RL-02, RL-11 and RL-22 in R1

*Entry written 2026-11-10 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** R1 includes one normalised event per call (RL-02), a
  callback the rail does not sign confirmed before it counts (RL-11) and
  Safaricom's SIM-swap and IMSI check before money moves (RL-22), which
  the rails' and the transactions' rules rely on.
- **Requires:** RL-02; RL-11; RL-22; docs/delivery/delivery-plan.md "RL-02 (one normalised event per call)".
- **Argued (owner):** "bring RL-02, RL-11 and RL-22 into R1".
- **Argued against (inferred):** leaving them for later, and saying what
  holds in R1 without them: callbacks acted on unproved, and payouts to a
  number whose SIM may have changed.
- **Costs (inferred):** three more capabilities built for R1, within the
  v1 cap, as all three were already phase 1; Safaricom's SIM-swap query
  to be granted to us as an authorised partner (FS-386).
