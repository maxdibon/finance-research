# Decisions, 1000 to 1099

8 decisions, in id order. How an entry is written, and why, is
[the index](../decisions.md).

## FS-1004 · A borrower consents to each partner apart

*Entry written 2026-10-27 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** a borrower's consent to a partner's data being used is
  given for each partner apart by default; an institution may set a
  product's partner terms to one consent for the partners named at that
  moment, never future ones, or turn consent off only where another
  lawful basis is recorded by decision with its impact assessment.
- **Requires:** MF-42; docs/specs/lending-service.md §5.
- **Argued (owner):** "i think the more restrictive is that retailers
  consent per distributor, unless this is waived or turned off".
- **Argued against:** one consent for every partner, present and future,
  which is not specific enough to stand as consent.
- **Costs (inferred):** a consent asked at each new relationship; the
  basis behind "off" settled with counsel (LQ-22).

## FS-1010 · Virtual cards, through a sponsor bank and an issuer processor

*Entry written 2026-10-27 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** virtual cards come in through partners: the institution
  issues under a sponsor bank's BIN with an issuer processor; a card
  draws on a deposit account or a credit line, each authorisation asked
  of us in real time; card numbers stay with the processor, never stored
  or seen by us; the scheme's settlement reconciled by recon. Physical
  cards and card processing stay theirs.
- **Requires:** OC-25.
- **Argued (owner):** "what about supporting virtual cards, onit was
  interested in this".
- **Argued against:** processing cards ourselves, which would bring PCI
  DSS's full scope and scheme membership; leaving cards out altogether,
  as the product landscape first said.
- **Costs (inferred):** a sponsor bank and a processor per market; a
  real-time authorisation path with the deposits service's availability;
  the approvals in LQ-23.

## FS-1018 · One identity per person, per institution; roles as profiles; staff apart

*Entry written 2026-10-27 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** one identity per person, per institution, never one across
  institutions; each role a profile granted by its owner (themselves, a
  business under its mandate, a partner within the institution's
  limits); a business's first administrators those its board resolution
  names, checked against the registry; a partner's field agent in a
  profile of their own identity; staff in the staff realm, linked to
  their own customer identity by ID number for conflict checks only.
- **Requires:** ID-25, ID-26; docs/market/channels.md.
- **Argued (owner):** "agreed on all four, go ahead".
- **Argued against:** a login per role, which hides conflicts between
  roles; one identity across institutions, which joins data two
  institutions own; staff and customer as one identity, which weakens
  separation of duties where fraud happens.
- **Costs (inferred):** profiles and organisations to build in access
  and every channel; a board resolution for each business, from a
  template (FS-1019); the link's privacy line kept true.

## FS-1052 · One live edition per job, while more than one runs

*Entry written 2026-10-29 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** a second ERP, or a second version of one, runs in three
  shapes: each institution on its own edition through one gateway
  release carrying every adapter, the contract suite run nightly for
  every edition in use; two products in one instance, each job in one
  of them and the person with one home; old and new side by side for a
  port or an upgrade, the target taking no calls and publishing
  nothing until the switch. In every shape, one live edition per job.
  An upgrade never changes the old in place, so falling back stays safe
  until staff are let into the new; after that, a fault is fixed
  forward.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/back-office.md §3 "One live edition per job";
  docs/runbooks/odoo-upgrade.md.
- **Argued (owner):** "update the back-office spec to show how a second
  erp or different versions of the erp running".
- **Argued against (inferred):** writing to the old and the new at once
  during a port, so either could be fallen back to at any time, which
  makes every record a reconciliation and every event a possible
  duplicate; a gateway for each institution, which makes each release a
  fork; upgrading production in place, which leaves nothing to fall
  back to.
- **Costs (inferred):** two editions to run, size and license for the
  weeks of a rehearsal (LQ-31); the suite run against every edition in
  use, each night; a branch of our Odoo modules for each version in use;
  once staff are let in, no way back but forward.

## FS-1055 · The staff workspace acts as the person, by token exchange

*Entry written 2026-10-30 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** standard token exchange in R1, for the staff workspace only:
  the `odoo` client exchanges the signed-in person's token for one
  addressed to the lending service or the approval engine alone, still
  naming the person. An exchanged token never counts as step-up; a
  decision still sends the person to Keycloak for a fresh second factor.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/access-keycloak.md "Token exchange, for the staff workspace only"; docs/specs/access-keycloak.md "K15".
- **Argued (owner):** "go ahead with all of them", on the review's
  recommendation.
- **Argued against (inferred):** passing the person's Odoo token through,
  which would have the lending service accept tokens addressed to Odoo;
  a service account with a header naming the user, which trusts Odoo's
  word for who acted, so a compromised Odoo could act as anyone.
- **Costs (inferred):** one more Keycloak feature to configure and check
  (K15); where OCA's auth_oidc keeps tokens is to confirm, since Odoo's
  own OAuth module stores the access token on the user record.

## FS-1056 · One name per component, in the form each place allows

*Entry written 2026-10-30 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** prose names a component in words; a diagram's box in
  capitals; an identifier in kebab-case, the component's id, or in
  snake_case where a hyphen is not allowed; events by product. Kapu MFI
  and Kapu Sacco are products, named in words only; the service is the
  lending service, and its Keycloak client is `lending-service`.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/README.md "One name per component"; docs/specs/README.md "A product we sell (Kapu MFI, Kapu Sacco) is named in words only".
- **Argued (owner):** "we had a naming scheme, something like
  lending_service for components, should we use that across the
  documentation?"; "go ahead with all of them".
- **Argued against (inferred):** snake_case in prose, against the design
  system's plain words; one form everywhere, which Kubernetes names and
  PostgreSQL identifiers cannot both take.
- **Costs (inferred):** an Id column to keep current; every spec's prose
  read for the service named as its product.

## FS-1057 · A loan's events in its product's domain

*Entry written 2026-10-30 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** the lending service publishes a loan's events in
  `kapu.mfi` under lender rules and `kapu.sacco` under Sacco rules,
  chosen by the rule set the product version names; the same fact keeps
  the same name in both; each rule set's own events stay in its domain.
- **Requires:** docs/specs/event-bus-kafka.md "Loans: the domain by product".
- **Argued (owner):** "i prefer separating kapu.mfi and sacco, so that
  can correctly attribute the events".
- **Argued against (inferred):** one `kapu.lending` domain for both,
  which loses whose product a fact belongs to; a domain chosen by the
  institution, which breaks for one licensed for both.
- **Costs (inferred):** consumers read two topics from R4; the same
  names kept in step across both domains.

## FS-1072 · A signed document, kept in both the vault and the signing tool

*Entry written 2026-11-01 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** the one exception to "a file never sits in both": a signed
  document and its certificate stay in the signing tool (Odoo Sign) as
  the signature's own evidence, and a copy is filed in the vault, which
  every other system and person reads.
- **Requires:** docs/architecture/README.md §6 "a signed document, in both"; docs/specs/vault.md "The one exception, by"; docs/specs/back-office.md "one file kept in both".
- **Argued (owner):** "let's confirm and note the exception".
- **Argued against (inferred):** only the certificate in the signing
  tool, which leaves the signature unverifiable in its own tool and
  depends on a tool's ability to drop a signed file; only the signing
  tool's copy, which puts personal contracts in Odoo for every user with
  access (FS-527) and is lost when the ERP changes (back-office rule 6).
- **Costs (inferred):** two copies of every signed document, each on its
  own retention: the vault deletes its copy on its clock, and the
  signing tool's copy must be deleted on the same clock, which the
  back office is asked to do.

## FS-1077 · Several identity providers behind one check: aggregators and direct sources

*Entry written 2026-11-01 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** each identity check (a national ID, a KRA PIN, a business's
  registration, a phone number, PEP and sanctions screening, liveness)
  as one contract in our words, with an adapter per provider: direct
  sources such as NPDM (formerly IPRS), KRA GavaConnect and the business
  registry, and aggregators such as Prembly and Smile ID. Each
  institution's checks are routed per check, a primary and a fallback;
  every result names the provider that gave it.
- **Requires:** docs/modules/identity.md "Providers: aggregators and direct sources"; docs/delivery/onboarding.md "OB-COM-27"; docs/architecture/README.md §6 "Several providers behind each check".
- **Argued (owner):** "the plan is to support multiple identity
  aggregators as well as direct integrations with providers like iprs".
- **Argued against (inferred):** one vendor for every check, which ties
  every institution to its coverage, prices and outages, and leaves no
  route where an institution holds its own register agreement; direct
  integrations only, which leaves out liveness, PEP screening and the
  documents no register covers.
- **Costs (inferred):** an adapter, contract tests and terms for each
  provider; each provider named as a processor in every institution's
  agreement and privacy notice; routing to configure and watch for each
  institution.

## FS-1078 · Identity checks chained, per product

*Entry written 2026-11-01 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** each product's identity checks as a chain: its steps in
  order, each with its own provider route (FS-1077), each passing on
  only what the next step needs. The first chain: the phone number
  confirmed with the operator (Safaricom), then the person on the
  national register (NPDM), then liveness against the register's photo.
- **Requires:** docs/modules/identity.md "Checks are chained, per product".
- **Argued (owner):** "include that providers can be chained, so i start
  with safaricom to confirm the mobile number and details, then go npdm,
  then close with liveliness".
- **Argued against (inferred):** every check run side by side, which asks
  the person for everything at once, cannot use one step's answer in the
  next, and pays for a liveness check on someone the register has
  already refused.
- **Costs (inferred):** a slower check when each step waits on the one
  before; one provider's answer handed to the next, which LQ-36 asks
  about; chains to configure and test for each product.

## FS-1093 · The hosting provider's managed firewall, in front of our edge

*Entry written 2026-11-05 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** a managed firewall from the hosting provider in front of
  every instance, with its flood protection: from the internet, only the
  edge's listeners are reachable, and everything else, administration
  included, is closed before it reaches us. Our edge stays the first
  thing that reads a request.
- **Requires:** docs/modules/platform-security.md "the hosting provider's managed firewall"; docs/architecture/flows.md "managed firewall"; docs/settings/edge.md "managed firewall".
- **Argued (owner):** "update the design to include the provider's
  managed firewall, while it might be azure firewall, i'm confident liquid
  provide this service".
- **Argued against (inferred):** our edge alone facing the internet,
  which leaves every port the cluster opens by mistake reachable; Azure
  Firewall by name, which Azure Stack Hub does not offer.
- **Costs (inferred):** one more party in the path, its rules changed
  through the provider and kept in step with the edge's listeners; its
  terms in the hosting contract.

## FS-1094 · Authenticator apps for every actor

*Entry written 2026-11-05 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** an authenticator app as a way to sign in for every actor:
  staff as before, and customers too, offered at the first sign-in once
  an SMS code has proved the bound phone, and their default once
  registered; SMS codes kept for customers without a smartphone; a lost
  app removed only after the customer is verified again.
- **Requires:** docs/settings/keycloak.md "an authenticator app, offered at the first sign-in"; docs/specs/access-keycloak.md "a code from an authenticator app".
- **Argued (owner):** "let's include authenticator apps for all actors".
- **Argued against (inferred):** SMS codes alone for customers, which a
  SIM swap or an intercepted message defeats; an app required of every
  customer, which shuts out borrowers on feature phones.
- **Costs (inferred):** an enrolment step and help for customers new to
  authenticator apps; two ways to sign in to support and test; recovery
  through a fresh identity check when a phone is lost.

## FS-1102 · Every common browser

*Entry written 2026-11-05 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** every screen working in the current and previous versions
  of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on computers and phones, tested in
  each before release; customer pages on light browsers too; no control
  that depends on one browser.
- **Requires:** docs/market/channels.md "Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari"; docs/templates/ict-policy.md "Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari".
- **Argued (owner):** "we need to be cross browser".

  kinds of device; features held to what all four support.

## FS-1103 · All three mobile operators

*Entry written 2026-11-05 by Claude for Eric (Assistant), from the owner's words in chat.*

- **Chose:** Safaricom, Airtel and Telkom treated alike: the identity
  chain's first step asks the customer's own operator, and every channel
  and policy names all three and their mobile money where the
  institution collects through them.
- **Requires:** docs/modules/identity.md "Safaricom, Airtel or Telkom"; docs/templates/delivery-channels.md "Airtel Money".

  collects through it.
