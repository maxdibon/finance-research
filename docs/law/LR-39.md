# Law of Succession Act (Cap. 160)

The law register's row is [LR-39](../regulation.md); this card says what
a reader of any of our documents needs to know about it, once (FS-868).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Citation | The Law of Succession Act (Cap. 160) |
| Regulator | None: the courts grant representation and supervise estates |
| Also called | Succession Act |
| Made under | An Act of Parliament |
| In force | Yes |
| What it is | Who inherits when someone dies, and how their estate is administered: grants of representation, their confirmation, and the representatives' powers and duties |

## Provisions we cite

| Provision | What it requires |
|---|---|
| s. 3 | Its definitions: a personal representative is the executor or administrator of a deceased person; the estate is their free property; a child includes one conceived but not yet born |
| s. 45 | No one may take possession of, dispose of, or otherwise intermeddle with a deceased person's free property except as the Act, another law or a grant of representation allows: an offence, and answerable to the rightful representative |
| s. 54 | The court may limit a grant of representation, in the forms the Fifth Schedule lists |
| s. 55 | No grant, limited or not, allows capital assets to be distributed until it is confirmed under s. 71 |
| s. 71 | After six months, or less if the court directs, the holder of a grant applies to the court to confirm it, so the capital can be distributed |
| s. 79 | The executor or administrator granted representation is the deceased's personal representative, and the estate's property vests in them, subject to any limit on the grant |
| s. 92 | A person who pays or transfers in good faith under a grant of representation is protected, even if the grant is later found defective or revoked |
