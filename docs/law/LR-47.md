# Land Act (Cap. 280)

The law register's row is [LR-47](../regulation.md); this card says what
a reader of any of our documents needs to know about it, once (FS-868).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Citation | The Land Act (Cap. 280), formerly No. 6 of 2012 |
| Regulator | None: the courts apply it; the National Land Commission for public land |
| Also called | Land Act |
| Made under | An Act of Parliament |
| In force | Yes |
| What it is | How land is held, leased and charged; Part VII sets how a charge over land is created, discharged and enforced, which the lender regulations defer to for land security (LR-03 reg 30(3)) |

## Provisions we cite

| Provision | What it requires |
|---|---|
| s. 79 | A charge is made by an instrument in the prescribed form (79(1)); a charge of a matrimonial home is valid only if the spouse living there also signs or assents (79(3)); a formal charge takes effect only once registered, and gives no remedy until it is (79(5)); holding the title deed alone is an informal charge, a lien by deposit of documents, enforced only by a court's order (79(6), (7)) |
| s. 80 | A charge is security only, never a transfer of the land (80(1)); every charge instrument states the terms of sale, what default leads to, and the chargor's reliefs (80(3)) |
| s. 85 | The chargor may discharge the charge by paying everything owed at any time before a sale (85(1)); a term that takes away or fetters that right is void (85(2)); the instrument may ask for one month's notice, or at most one month's interest, on an early discharge (85(3)) |
| s. 88 | Covenants implied in every charge: to pay principal and interest when due, pay the land's rates and outgoings, keep buildings in repair, insure them against fire in the chargor's and chargee's joint names, and not lease beyond a year or transfer without the chargee's consent (88(1)) |
| s. 90 | After one month in default, a written notice to the chargor (90(1)) stating the default, the amount to pay and a time of at least three months to pay it, or two months for any other default, what follows, and the right to apply to court (90(2)); if not met within ninety days of service, the chargee may sue, appoint a receiver, lease, take possession or sell (90(3)) |
| s. 96 | A sale only once the time in the s. 90 notice has passed in default (96(1)); then a notice to sell, and no contract of sale completed until forty days after it is served (96(2)); copies to the spouse who consented, co-owners, lessees, other chargees and guarantors, and posted at the land (96(3)) |
| s. 97 | The chargee owes the chargor, guarantors and later chargees a duty to get the best price reasonably obtainable (97(1)); a forced sale valuation by a valuer before selling (97(2)); a sale 25% or more below market value is presumed a breach, and the chargor may ask a court to void it (97(3)); none of this can be contracted out of (97(7)) |
| s. 101 | Sale proceeds go first to the land's rates and outgoings, then any prior charge, then the costs of the sale, then what the charge secures, then later charges; any balance to the chargor |
| s. 102 | Until the land is sold, the chargor may discharge the charge by paying all it secures, and the chargee then delivers the discharge and the title documents |
| s. 103 | The chargor, a joint chargor or a spouse whose consent was needed but not given may apply to court for relief from any remedy, at any time after the s. 90 notice is served |
