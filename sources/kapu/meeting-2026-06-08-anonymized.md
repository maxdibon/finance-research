## Summary

A strategy session to align the product pillars and set development
priorities for the banking, gateway and circle products.

**Product pillars.** The meeting set out the roadmaps for three pillars. The
infrastructure supports deposit mobilisation for tier-2 and tier-3 banks,
merchant payment aggregation, and loan management.

**Phasing.** Development puts Kapu Collect and the Kapu Pay Gateway ahead of
everything else. Active development of Kapu Pay Circle stops until a paying
customer is secured.

**Positioning.** Success depends on presenting an integrated ecosystem to
target clients, not siloed products. Security and fraud detection have to
be designed into the platform from the start.

## Decisions

**Aligned**

- **Ecosystem positioning.** Market the offering on the whole ecosystem —
  reconciliation, monitoring, messaging and CRM — so that the company is
  seen as a technology provider and not a standalone payment service.
- **Roadmap order.** Kapu Collect first, then the Kapu Pay Gateway, then
  Kapu Circle.

**Shelved**

- **Kapu Circle** — active development stops until a specific customer
  commits.

## Details

- **Purpose (00:05:44).** The product lead opened the meeting to discuss
  Kapu's strategic direction through three product pillars: Kapu Collect,
  Kapu Pay Gateway and Kapu Pay Circle. The aim was to align the team on the
  roadmap, the implementation plan for each pillar, and the dependencies
  development needs.
- **The pillars (00:07:05, 00:08:21).** **Kapu Collect:** deposit
  mobilisation for tier-2 and tier-3 banks. **Kapu Pay Gateway:** an
  aggregator giving merchants a single API. **Kapu Pay Circle:** solutions
  for circles, including loan and member management. All three use the Kapu
  platform as their backend.
- **Collect and Gateway (00:10:11).** Collect is deployed inside the bank's
  own infrastructure. Banks pay a licence fee to expose APIs for deposits
  and account management. The Gateway is a single API for merchants
  covering M-Pesa, Airtel Money, T-Cash, PesaLink and cards, with a merchant
  dashboard and a reconciliation engine.
- **Circle (00:11:27).** Manages member accounts, payments and the full
  loan lifecycle: repayment periods, charges and interest rates. Loan
  management sits on Fineract, configured to each circle's environment.
- **Phasing (00:12:48, 00:14:31).** Phase 1: Kapu Collect. Phase 2: the
  Gateway, starting with M-Pesa and Airtel Money. Phase 3: cards and
  PesaLink, then Kapu Pay Circle. Later: Odoo integration, AI, credit
  scoring and fraud detection.
- **Pre-launch dependencies (00:15:42).** Complete the Safaricom go-live;
  set up trust accounts with the PesaLink sponsor bank; secure a PSP
  licence; finish the IPRS integration for onboarding checks.
- **International transfers (00:16:58).** The architect raised
  international money transfer: research stablecoins and focus on niche
  markets, such as Kenyan cruise-ship workers who struggle to send money
  home. The product lead noted there is no firm plan yet; candidate
  gateways are still being explored.
- **Channels (00:20:55).** The architect noted that T-Cash was missing from
  the presentation. The product lead confirmed it can sit alongside the
  other mobile-money channels.
- **Circle go-to-market (00:22:13, 00:24:52).** The commercial lead noted
  the politics of selling to circles. The suggested entry point was a
  narrow product, such as an Odoo-based reconciliation tool. The team
  agreed the existing Circle demo is strong enough for pitching, and parked
  development until a paying customer is identified.
- **Gateway direction (00:26:03, 00:27:16, 00:30:12).** The commercial lead
  stressed that Kapu must be the first consumer of its own Gateway. Routing
  internal products, including Odoo, through it proves the product, auto-
  reconciliation included, before external merchants use it.
- **The wider ecosystem (00:28:28, 00:42:36).** The architect proposed
  presenting the whole ecosystem — reconciliation platforms, Grafana
  monitoring, messaging and CRM — as the advantage over competitors who
  offer siloed products.
- **Payment links and settlement (00:31:37, 00:32:50).** The commercial
  lead proposed payment links, such as invoices sent by SMS. The long-term
  goal is a PSP licence, so that the company can offer settlement services
  to merchants directly, for example a prospective merchant client.
- **Regulation and benchmarks (00:34:14, 00:37:20).** Competing social
  fintechs partner with banks to offer banking-as-a-service without a CBK
  licence of their own. The commercial lead stressed that the licence must
  not hold up product development: start with mobile money, and add
  bank-integrated services later.
- **End-to-end suite (00:41:14).** Because the company already has payroll,
  HR and CRM systems, it can pitch an end-to-end solution to tier-2 and
  tier-3 clients, many of whom still work manually.
- **Fraud and security (00:42:36).** Engineer B and the architect stressed
  that fraud detection, monitoring and reporting must be in the platform
  design from the start. That gives a better product than retrofitting
  them.
- **Sprint planning (00:54:39, 00:57:08).** Immediate development focuses on
  completing Kapu Collect and the Kapu Pay Gateway. The product lead
  coordinates sprint planning; the commercial lead keeps looking for bank
  and merchant leads.
